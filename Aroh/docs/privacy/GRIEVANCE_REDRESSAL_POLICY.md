## 1. Statutory Mandate & Purpose

Under **Section 13 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** and **Rule 14 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025**, every Data Fiduciary must establish an easily accessible, effective, and transparent grievance redressal mechanism to resolve complaints raised by Data Principals.

This policy governs how grievances are received, acknowledged, investigated, resolved, and documented by AROH.

### 3.1 Submission Requirements
A grievance may be submitted by any Data Principal or their lawful representative. Submissions must include:
1. Complainant's name and contact email.
2. Category of grievance (e.g., unauthorized processing, consent withdrawal failure, rights denial, delayed response, security incident concern).
3. Clear factual description of the issue and relevant account details.

### 3.2 Timelines for Redressal
- **Acknowledgement:** Within **twenty-four (24) hours** of receipt.
- **Initial Triage:** Within **forty-eight (48) hours**.
- **Final Substantive Resolution:** Promptly and within **thirty (30) days** of receipt. In no event shall resolution exceed the statutory limit of **ninety (90) days** prescribed by the DPDP Rules, 2025.

---

## 4. Escalation to the Data Protection Board of India

If AROH fails to resolve your grievance within the prescribed statutory period, or if you are dissatisfied with the resolution provided by our Grievance Redressal Officer, you have the statutory right under **Section 13(3) of the DPDP Act** to escalate your complaint to the **Data Protection Board of India**.

- **Appellate Authority:** Data Protection Board of India
- **Statutory Grounds for Appeal:** Failure to respond within statutory timeframe, unsatisfactory grievance resolution, or ongoing violation of the DPDP Act or Rules.
